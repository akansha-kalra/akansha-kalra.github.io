Research Interests

My research aims to develop AI agents that can reliably learn and adapt to human objectives from preferences and demonstrations. While learning from human feedback offers a promising alternative to manually specifying objectives and behaviors, it does not guarantee alignment with human intent. An agent may optimize a misaligned reward without exhibiting obvious failures, a policy learned from demonstrations may be vulnerable to adversarial manipulation, and feedback aggregated across users may obscure legitimate differences in individual objectives.

My PhD tackles these challenges along three complementary directions:

  • Interpreting what agents learn: Developing interpretable reward representations to identify unintended objectives and diagnose silent misalignment.
  • Understanding when learned behavior fails: Investigating adversarial vulnerabilities in imitation learning and the reliability of learned policies.
  • Personalizing whose preferences agents serve: Learning individualized rewards and policies from heterogeneous crowd feedback without requiring annotator identities.

My long-term vision is to bridge the gap between learning from human feedback and reliably acting on human intent, enabling intelligent AI systems and robots that serve human objectives while simultaneously adapting to the complexity, uncertainty, and diversity of the real world.
